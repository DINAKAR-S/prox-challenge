# How To Choose A Welder — Selection Chart (single page)

Title: "HOW TO CHOOSE A WELDER"
Callout box (top right, red): "IMPORTANT! IDENTIFY YOUR INPUT VOLTAGE: DO YOU HAVE 120 VOLT OR 240 VOLT?"

The chart is a 4-column decision matrix. The four columns (processes), left to right, are:
**Column 1: FLUX-CORED / FCAW** · **Column 2: MIG / GMAW** · **Column 3: STICK / SMAW** · **Column 4: TIG / GTAW**

## Full decision matrix

| # | Question/Row | FLUX-CORED / FCAW | MIG / GMAW | STICK / SMAW | TIG / GTAW |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is your skill level? | **LOW** (highlighted) — options were Low/Moderate/High | **LOW** (highlighted) — options were Low/Moderate/High | **MODERATE** (highlighted) — options were Low/Moderate/High | **HIGH** (highlighted) — options were Low/Moderate/High |
| 2 | Will you need shielding gas? | NO-GAS REQUIRED | GAS REQUIRED (indoor welding recommended) | NO-GAS REQUIRED | GAS REQUIRED (indoor welding recommended) |
| 3 | What type of material will you be welding? | Steel, Stainless steel | Steel, Stainless steel, Aluminum (spool gun required) | Steel, Stainless steel, Castings | Steel, Stainless steel, Chrome moly → DC TIG required; Aluminum, Magnesium alloys → AC TIG required |
| 4 | What is your material thickness? | 18 Gauge to 5/16″ | 22 Gauge to 3/8″ | 10 Gauge to 1/2″ | 24 Gauge to 3/16″ |
| 5 | Typical applications | Galvanized steel · General fabrication · Pipe and tubing · Maintenance & repair | Sheet metal · Automotive body · Tubing · Structural steel | Pipe and tubing · Structural steel · Pressure vessels · Maintenance & repair | Stainless steel exhausts · Bicycle frames · Thin wall pipe & tubing · Metal art |
| 6 | How clean do you need your weld? | MORE SPATTER | CLEAN / MINIMAL SPATTER | MORE SPATTER | EXTREMELY CLEAN |
| → | Use this welding process | **FLUX-CORED / FCAW**: Ideal for outdoor or windy conditions; Forgiving on rusty or dirty steels; Good out of position welding capabilities; High deposition rates achievable | **MIG / GMAW**: Fast production (high welding speeds); Easiest to learn; Clean welds with no slag; Better control on thin materials | **STICK / SMAW**: Ideal for outdoor or windy conditions; Forgiving on rusty or dirty steels; Deep penetration; Good choice for thicker materials | **TIG / GTAW**: Highest quality welds; Extremely aesthetic weld appearance; Can be used on a variety of materials; Precise control |

Each process row in the "Use this welding process" band is preceded by a process icon and label: "FLUX-CORED/FCAW", "MIG/GMAW", "STICK/SMAW", "TIG/GTAW".

## Right sidebar: Duty Cycle explainer

"The Duty Cycle is the number of minutes, within a 10 minute period, a welding process can produce the specified welding current without overheating."

### DUTY CYCLE EXAMPLE
- 165A @ 30% Duty Cycle

- 7 Minutes Resting
