## Wire Weld Diagnosis – Workpiece Heat Control / Weld Penetration

Three cross-section profile views along a heat spectrum arrow ("Not hot enough" ←→ "Ideal heat" ←→ "Too hot"):

| Condition | Description |
|---|---|
| INADEQUATE PENETRATION | Not hot enough — weld bead sits on the surface, joint below shows a visible unfused gap/crack down the middle |
| PROPER PENETRATION | Ideal heat — weld penetrates fully through the joint, forming a solid triangular fused profile |
| EXCESS PENETRATION OR BURN-THROUGH | Too hot — weld sags well below the surface line, forming a deep rounded-bottom penetration profile |

**How to increase workpiece heat and increase penetration (to weld THICKER workpieces properly):**
a. Increase weld current
b. Decrease travel speed
c. Use faster wire feed
d. Use shorter CTWD

**How to reduce workpiece heat and limit penetration (to weld THINNER workpieces properly):**
e. Decrease weld current
f. Increase travel speed
g. Use slower wire feed
h. Use longer CTWD

## Example Wire Weld Diagrams

Six side-by-side bead panels, each showing a cross-section profile (top) and a top-view bead photo/illustration (bottom):

### Figure: Good Weld
Top-view: smooth, uniform, tightly-overlapped rippled bead of consistent width, rounded bead end. Cross-section: bead sits flush with slight even crown, symmetric. No correction needed — this is the reference weld.

### Figure: Voltage Too Low or Wire Feed Too Slow
Top-view: bead is narrow, tall/humped, with a small rounded ball-like blob at the end — piled up rather than flat. Cross-section: bead sits high/proud on the surface (small convex bump). TO CORRECT: Increase Output Voltage or Increase Wire Feed Speed.

### Figure: Voltage Too High or Wire Feed Too Fast
Top-view: bead is wide, irregular, splashed-looking with scattered spatter dots on both sides and a flared/frayed pattern. Cross-section: wide flat bead. TO CORRECT: Decrease Output Voltage or Decrease Wire Feed Speed.

### Figure: Travel Speed Too Fast
Top-view: narrow, elongated bead with a fine zig-zag/herringbone ripple pattern, thin and stringy. Cross-section: small, narrow bead, minimal buildup. TO CORRECT: travel slower. (Icon: MIG gun angled, arrow labeled "travel slower" pointing along the workpiece.)

### Figure: Travel Speed Too Slow
Top-view: wide, heavy, blobby bead with a large rounded pooled end — excess material piled up. Cross-section: wide, thick, overly built-up bead. TO CORRECT: travel faster. (Icon: MIG gun angled, arrow labeled "travel faster" pointing along the workpiece.)

### Figure: CTWD Too Long or Wrong Polarity
Top-view: bead covered in scattered spatter/porosity dots along its whole length plus a rounded end blob — irregular and dirty-looking. Cross-section: small irregular bead. TO CORRECT: Check Polarity and maintain less than 1/2" CTWD. (Icon: MIG gun angled steeply downward into the workpiece with an arrow showing the short "less than 1/2\" CTWD" gun-to-work distance.)
